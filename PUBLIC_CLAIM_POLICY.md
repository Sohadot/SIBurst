# Public Claim Policy

A fail-safe policy for every statement published under the SIBurst name —
in this repository, on any future site, and in any material derived from them.

The policy exists because the subject invites overstatement. A framework about
transitions in intelligence is only credible if it is strict about what it does
not know.

## Claim classes

Every public statement belongs to one of five classes.

| Class | What it is | Standard it must meet | Example form |
|---|---|---|---|
| **DEFINITION** | Framework-defined terminology. | Consistent with `NAME_ARCHITECTURE.md` and `GLOSSARY.md`. True by stipulation inside the framework, and never presented as more. | "Within SIBurst, *burst* denotes discontinuity." |
| **OBSERVATION** | Externally documented factual observation. | Supported by an authoritative public source, cited where it appears. If sourcing is unnecessary to the argument, remove the claim instead. | "[Source] reports that…" |
| **INTERPRETATION** | Reasoned reading of evidence. | Labelled as a reading; the evidence it rests on is identifiable; alternative readings are not excluded by assertion. | "One way to read this is as an oversight mismatch." |
| **SCENARIO** | A possible future condition. | Framed as conditional ("if", "could", "one possibility"). No dates, probabilities, or inevitability unless separately sourced. | "If agents begin to…, a threshold could…" |
| **METAPHOR** | Explanatory conceptual language. | Recognizable as figurative; never used as evidence. | "Phase change", borrowed from physics. |

## The separation rule

Public materials must make the class of a claim clear whenever confusion is
possible. In particular:

- A **definition** must not be presented as an **observation**.
  ("SIBurst defines X" is not "research shows X".)
- A **metaphor** must not be used as evidence for an **interpretation**.
  (Physical phase transitions do not show that AI capability has thresholds.)
- A **scenario** must not drift into the indicative mood.
  ("Could" must not become "will".)
- An **interpretation** must not be stated as settled fact.

Where a single passage mixes classes, the transitions between them should be
visible to the reader.

## Prohibited claims

The following are prohibited in all public SIBurst materials:

1. **Achieved superintelligence without evidence** — no statement that
   superintelligence exists, or that a named system is superintelligent, absent
   governed, authoritative evidence meeting the OBSERVATION standard.
2. **Unsupported AGI or SI dates** — no predicted dates or time windows for AGI,
   superintelligence, or any specific transition.
3. **Unsupported inevitability** — no statement that a transition must,
   necessarily, or certainly occur.
4. **Unsupported capability numbers** — no quantitative capability figures
   without a cited source.
5. **Arbitrary scores** — no intelligence scores, multipliers, or indices
   invented for effect.
6. **Sensational singularity claims** — no rhetoric of arrival, runaway, or
   "the singularity is here".
7. **Fabricated market statistics** — no invented figures about markets,
   adoption, or industry size.
8. **Conceptual definitions presented as scientific consensus** — the SIBurst
   framework is a lens, not a consensus position, and must not be described as
   one.

Interface language (see `SITE_ARCHITECTURE.md`) such as "TRANSITION DETECTED"
describes states of the interface, not facts about the world. Where that could
be misread, the interface must make the distinction clear.

## Review questions

Before anything is published, ask:

1. Which class is this claim?
2. Does it meet that class's standard?
3. Could a reasonable reader mistake it for a stronger class?
4. If it were quoted alone, out of context, would it still be accurate?

## The rule

> If a claim sounds stronger than the evidence behind it,
> weaken the claim — not the standard.
